# Review of `e_GMM-scaling` — 2026-11-25

Scope: the e-GMM project (Tier 1, candidate 2 of *Research Novelty Report: e-means vs k-means*), the source paper it extends (Bessa et al., *e-means: outperforming k-means (in one-tenth the epochs)*, arXiv 2611.09501), and every script, PoC, experiment, log and report in this repository as of commit `0891c97`.
The fixes made in response are listed in [2026-11-25-FIX-PLAN.md](./2026-11-25-FIX-PLAN.md) and in the *Fix log* at the end of this file.

Line numbers below refer to commit `0891c97`, before the fixes. They may have moved since.

**Summary.** e-GMM is a faithful port of the e-means recipe to soft EM, and the code is readable. However:

- The core update rule is Cappé–Moulines online EM with a cosine schedule. Any novelty therefore has to come from the scaling laws and from evidence that component revival helps at scale.
- Before this review, nothing had run beyond NumPy on the CPU, on small synthetic data.
- The evidence for the two headline claims does not hold up: that revival is required, and that the α₀ law needs a d term. An independent 10-seed re-check reproduces the problems.

---

## 1. The base: what the e-means paper establishes

### Algorithm (Section 3, Algorithm 1)
- **Statistics.** Each cluster keeps an exponentially decayed count N_j and sum S_j. The centroid is read out as S_j / N_j and is never stored. Initialization sets N_j = 1e-10 and S_j = N_j · c_j⁽⁰⁾.
- **Step size.** α = 1 − γ follows a cosine schedule from α₀ down to ε·α₀, with ε = 0.1 (Eq. 5).
- **Dead-cluster revival (Appendix H).** Every 5 batches, clusters whose count falls below τ = 0.1 of the mean count are rebuilt by splitting a donor. The donor is drawn with probability proportional to N_j. Its centroid is perturbed multiplicatively by an alternating ±δ pattern, with δ = BF16 machine epsilon, and its count is halved. This follows the Faiss procedure.
- **Finalization.** Last-epoch mean (favours training MSE), or a slow EMA of centroids (favours test MSE).

### Scaling laws (Section 3.3, Table 2)

| Law | c | k | B | n | T | OOD regret | LOSO regret | R² |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| step α₀, Eq. 6 | 0.824 | −0.46 | 0.90 | −0.45 | −0.11 | 0.07 % | 0.07 % | 0.95 |
| batch B, knee at +0.2 % | 0.035 | 0.13 | — | 0.88 | 0.81 | 0.19 % | 0.20 % | 0.96 |
| batch B, knee ÷ 8 (n > 2M) | 0.0044 | 0.13 | — | 0.88 | 0.81 | 0.07 % | 0.06 % | n/a |

- Both laws are fitted on DEEP only, from 75k runs covering n ∈ [10⁵, 10⁸], k ∈ [256, 65 536] and n/k ≥ 8.
- The batch law is fitted only on runs that use the α₀ predicted by the decay law.
- Regret is the relative MSE gap to the best hyperparameter for that configuration.

### Ablation (Table 1: DEEP, n = 10⁶, k = 16 384, T = 3, 30 seeds)
- Dead-cluster revival has by far the largest effect: +2.97 % train MSE and +2.24 % test MSE without it.
- Resampling dead clusters from the batch recovers only part of that loss.
- The cosine schedule, the finalization step and k-means++ initialization each matter by a fraction of a percent.

### Two passages that matter directly for e-GMM
- **Appendix B.1:** "We tried using several different datasets in the law to include an exponent in d, but found this exponent to be particularly unstable and decided to drop it."
  - Section 3.3 adds that the quantity that matters is the data's *local intrinsic dimension*, which is generally unknown.
- **Related work:** the EMA centroid rule "is a hard-assignment form of EM with decayed statistics (Nowlan, 1991; Neal and Hinton, 1998)."

## 2. What the novelty report asks for (Tier 1, candidate 2)
- A three-statistic EMA (N_j, S_j, Q_j), with covariance read out above a floor λ₀·I_d.
- A dimension-aware step law: α₀ = C·k^a_k·B^a_B·n^a_n·T^a_T·d^a_d.
- Spectral splitting of a donor component along its principal eigenvector v₁.
- Datasets: SIFT1M, DEEP1B, DINOv2-100M and ImageNet-1k embeddings, with k ∈ [64, 65 536].
- Baselines: full-batch EM ("FAISS / scikit-learn") and stochastic EM (Cappé & Moulines).
- Metrics: test NLL, BIC/AIC, epochs to a target NLL, and the rate of collapsed Gaussians.
- Budget: 200–300 GPU-hours.

## 3. What the repository implemented, and what had actually run

### Pipeline
- **Core model.**
  - The E-step softmaxes the log-joint; the batch statistics (n_b, s_b, q_b) then update the EMA ([numpy_egmm.py:516](../../src/e_gmm/numpy_egmm.py#L516)).
  - Readout adds the floor to the covariance.
  - Every 5 steps, components with N_j < 0.1·mean are revived ([numpy_egmm.py:326](../../src/e_gmm/numpy_egmm.py#L326)).
  - Split strategies: `spectral_v1` (moment-preserving: μ ± δ√λ·v, Σ − δ²λ·vvᵀ, with δ = 0.5), `faiss_alt`, `random_orth`, `batch_resample` and `none`.
  - Finalization: `last_epoch`, `slow_ema` or `main_ema`.
- **Baselines.**
  - Cappé–Moulines online EM with a Robbins–Monro step size.
  - Full-batch EM, initialized from random points.
  - e-means followed by one hard covariance readout.
- **Other code.** A PyTorch mirror, a Triton kernel, and a low-rank + diagonal mode using Woodbury, with U updated by an Oja-style rule.
- **Runners.** PoC 1–4 feed [COMPILED-POC-REPORT.md](../../poc/results/COMPILED-POC-REPORT.md). Exp 00–04 feed [FINAL-EXPERIMENT-REPORT.md](../../experiments/results/FINAL-EXPERIMENT-REPORT.md), which carries the H1–H7 verdicts.

### What had actually run
- Only NumPy on the CPU, on synthetic data: n ≤ 4 800 in the PoCs and the experiment smoke runs, with d ≤ 64 and k ≤ 32.
- `torch` was not installed. The 4 torch tests were skipped, PoC-4's `torch_parity` is `null`, and Exp-04's torch benchmark reads "skipped".
- No real dataset had been used.
- The 21 NumPy unit tests pass.

## 4. Findings

### A. Novelty
- **The update rule is not new.** It is Cappé–Moulines online EM: Ŝ_t = (1 − γ_t)·Ŝ_{t−1} + γ_t·E[s(x)]. Only the step schedule differs: cosine instead of (t + t₀)^−β.
  - The repository's own primer, [02-Online-EM-Cappe-Moulines-and-Collapse.md](../basic-terms/02-Online-EM-Cappe-Moulines-and-Collapse.md), writes the same equation.
  - The e-means paper itself attributes EMA-statistics EM to Nowlan (1991) and Neal & Hinton (1998).
- **Related prior art** (Section 7) covers:
  - principal-axis splitting: SMEM, G-means
  - large-k GMMs: truncated variational EM by Lücke and colleagues
- **The "Flash-GMM" kernel is a standard reformulation.** It writes the diagonal Gaussian log-density as one GEMM on [x², x].
- **What remains new** is fitted step/batch laws for *soft* mini-batch EM, and evidence that revival helps at scale. Both are empirical claims, and the evidence so far is weak (B and C below).
- **The FAISS baseline does not exist.** The novelty report lists FAISS as a full-batch GMM baseline, but as far as we know FAISS has no GMM trainer.

### B. The PoC-2 evidence does not survive more seeds or a different step size
See Section 5.
- **The −2.8 nats "revival is mandatory" result comes from the hand-picked α₀ = 0.35.** At α₀ = 0.10, running with no splitting at all is within noise of spectral splitting.
- **In the original PoC-2 itself, e-GMM did not beat e-means + PostHoc** on the Zipf regime: 14.8957 vs 14.6991 test NLL. The compiled report's H2.1 sentence ("beats heuristic splits") leaves e-means + PostHoc out of the comparison.
- **The collapse is specific to e-GMM.** Plain online EM without revival did not collapse in the same setting (0.3 dead), and no method recorded a singular or floor event (all 0.0). The premise of "covariance collapse on mini-batches" was not demonstrated.

### C. The scaling law (the main novelty claim)
- **The T exponent is an artifact.** T is fixed at 3 in PoC-3 and Exp-01 ([poc_03:44](../../poc/poc_03_dimension_scaling_law.py#L44); `--epochs` default in [exp_01:67](../../experiments/exp_01_phase_a_scaling_laws.py#L67)).
  - So log T is collinear with the intercept, and the reported a_T is the minimum-norm least-squares split of the intercept.
  - In both runs a_T = 1.0986 × ln c exactly: 0.8325 = 1.0986 × ln 2.1336, and 0.5329 = 1.0986 × ln 1.6243.
  - [phase_a_scaling_manifest.json](../../configs/phase_a_scaling_manifest.json) varies T ∈ {2, 3, 5}, but the sweep script ignores it.
- **The batch law is empty.** Every Exp-01 knee landed at 512, the largest batch tried, so the observations are right-censored.
  - The reported R² = 1.0 comes from ss_tot = 0 meeting a 1e-12 denominator guard.
  - The PoC-3 knee fit has R² = 0.2353.
- **The d term does not help out of distribution.**
  - R² rises from 0.2753 to 0.7139 (PoC-3) and from 0.2969 to 0.7382 (Exp-01).
  - But the OOD NLL regret with and without d is 0.0230 vs 0.0243 nats (PoC-3) and 0.8859 vs 0.8861 nats (Exp-01).
  - Leave-one-dimension-out (LODO) α₀ error is still 89.44 % / 84.17 %, and leave-one-size-out (LOSO) error is 79.37 % / 69.76 %.
  - a_k = +0.19 / +0.15, the opposite sign to the paper's −0.46.
- **The sweep data is a single stretched Gaussian, not a mixture** (`generate_stretched_gaussian`).
  - `init_prior_mass = max(1, 0.25·B/k)` ties the initialization to B and k, which confounds a_B and a_k.
  - The α₀ grid has 9 coarse points, there are 2 seeds, and n ≤ 4 800.
- **In full mode, d is confounded with the data source.** [exp_01:92](../../experiments/exp_01_phase_a_scaling_laws.py#L92) loads DEEP (96-d) as `arr[:n, :d]`.
  - For d ∈ {256, 768} the shape check fails and the loader silently falls back to a synthetic Gaussian.
  - d then tracks "real vs synthetic", which is exactly the instability the e-means authors reported.
- **The code defaults are invented.** [schedule.py:54](../../src/e_gmm/schedule.py#L54) sets a_d = −0.25 and b_d = 0.05; neither was fitted.

### D. Benchmark design (Exp-02 full mode and the baselines)
- **Dimensions are truncated and data is silently replaced.**
  - [exp_02:104](../../experiments/exp_02_phase_b_billion_benchmarks.py#L104) keeps only the first 128 of 1024 dims for DINOv2 and of 768 for ImageNet.
  - If the file is missing, synthetic data runs under the real dataset's name.
  - Rows are taken from the start of the file rather than sampled.
  - The largest n is 1M, despite the "billion" framing.
- **The baselines are not matched or tuned.**
  - Online EM gets `cov_floor = 1e-8` and `init_sigma0 = 0.5`, against e-GMM's 1e-4 and 1.0.
  - Online EM is labelled "swept" but tries only β ∈ {0.60, 0.85} with t₀ = 10 fixed.
  - Full-batch EM starts from random points; scikit-learn defaults to k-means initialization.
  - The e-means baseline is one hard covariance readout, not e-means followed by EM.
  - e-GMM's α₀ = 0.35 is hand-set.
  - The e-means paper tuned its baselines on validation data.
- **Backends are mixed.** Baselines always run in NumPy on the CPU while e-GMM runs on torch/GPU in full mode, so H3's wall-clock claim is not comparable.
- **"Dead" (weight below 0.1× the mean) is a k-means notion.** For a GMM, small mixture weights can be correct.
  - On Stretched-PowerLaw-Diag, online EM has 10–10.67 "dead" components yet a better test NLL than e-GMM (21.4799 vs 21.5017).
  - Revival can therefore remove legitimate rare modes.
- **`floor_hit_rate` is ~0 by construction.** The floor is added to every variance rather than used as a clamp, so the rate is nonzero only when a raw variance is exactly zero. It reads 0.00 % even at λ₀ = 0.1.
- **The `last_epoch` readout is not "exact EM".** Its responsibilities come from parameters that move during the epoch.
  - In PoC-4 and Exp-03 it has *worse* test NLL than plain `main_ema`: 24.6447 vs 24.6154, and 24.6270 vs 24.5959.
- **BIC is computed on the test set**, which is non-standard.

### E. Report verdicts that contradict their own numbers
In both compilers the verdicts were hard-coded strings ("Supported"), not computed from the data.
- **H6 "Supported":** low_rank_r4 has BIC 21 078.9, worse than diag (20 868.6) and full (21 001.5). low_rank_diag also has the worst test NLL in both rotated suites (16.3403 and 20.7888).
- **H3 "Supported":**
  - FullBatchEM@25 beats e-GMM on Zipf-Rotated-Full: 14.5085 vs 14.6624.
  - Online EM beats e-GMM on Stretched-PowerLaw-Diag: 21.4799 vs 21.5017.
  - There are 3 seeds, with std 0.29–0.70.
- **H2 "Supported":** this is with 0.8859 nats of OOD regret and no pass threshold defined.
- **PoC H2.1 "beats heuristic splits":** this omits e-means + PostHoc, which beat spectral e-GMM (14.6991 vs 14.8957).
- **Top-m:** m = 16 (13.4946) and m = 8 (13.6315) both beat dense (13.6601), and in PoC-4 m = 4 beat dense by 0.29 nats (single seed). Noise exceeds the effect being measured.
- **The preliminary diagnostics agree with the critique.** DIAG 3 shows full-batch EM best on Overlapping-Rot (18.7143 vs e-GMM 18.7440), and e-means + PostHoc best on Zipf-Separated (14.2544 vs 14.2649).

### F. Systems ("Flash-GMM")
- **The Triton kernel ([triton_kernels.py:14](../../src/e_gmm/triton_kernels.py#L14)) is never called.** It computes only the log-sum-exp, not the responsibilities or the statistics the M-step needs.
  - Without top-m, the dense path materializes the full B × k matrix ([triton_kernels.py:146](../../src/e_gmm/triton_kernels.py#L146)).
- **The low-rank and full-covariance torch paths loop over every component in Python** ([torch_egmm.py:177](../../src/e_gmm/torch_egmm.py#L177), [:204](../../src/e_gmm/torch_egmm.py#L204), [:405](../../src/e_gmm/torch_egmm.py#L405)). The DINOv2 config has k = 16 384.
- **The low-rank factor U is updated by an Oja-style heuristic** ([numpy_egmm.py:274](../../src/e_gmm/numpy_egmm.py#L274)), not kept as an EMA sufficient statistic. The "three-statistic" story and the moment-preserving split do not hold in that mode.
- **The 456× speedup** compares a NumPy GEMM against a CPU broadcast that builds a B × k × d tensor. It says nothing about GPU performance.
- **Risk (unverified):** the optional BF16 GEMM on [x², x] can cancel catastrophically.
  - k-means only needs the argmin, which tolerates small errors.
  - GMM responsibilities and the NLL need accurate absolute logits.
- **Every GPU-hour figure is an unmeasured estimate**, because the torch path has never executed.

### Conclusions
1. **The no-revival collapse is caused by the step size, not by soft mini-batch EM.**
   - At α₀ = 0.35 it costs +2.24 nats (Zipf) and +2.95 nats (Overlap), with 13–15 of 16 components starved.
   - At α₀ = 0.10 it is within noise of spectral splitting in both regimes.
   - Changing the initial σ does not change this.
2. **Spectral beats Faiss-style splitting on Zipf** (+0.16 nats for faiss_alt, t = 2.84) but not on Overlap (t = 0.76).
3. **On Zipf, e-GMM is significantly better only than faiss_alt and its own collapsed runs.**
   - e-means → EM, matched online EM and e-means + PostHoc are slightly ahead of it, but not significantly (t between −1.2 and −1.9).
4. **On Overlap, e-GMM ties full-batch EM (+0.0028) and significantly beats online EM and both e-means pipelines.** This is a real but small positive result.
5. **The hand-set α₀ = 0.35 is not near-optimal.** α₀ = 0.10 improves Overlap by 0.20 nats.

## 6. What holds
- The float64 algebraic identities are correct: the [x², x] GEMM, Woodbury with the determinant lemma, the PSD floor, and moment conservation under the spectral split.
- The spectral split preserves the parent component's mean and covariance exactly whenever the floor does not bind.
- The NumPy unit tests pass and the code is readable.
- The Overlap-regime advantage over online EM and over the hard-assignment pipelines reproduces at 10 seeds.

## 7. Prior art to position against (from memory — verify before citing)
- **O. Cappé and E. Moulines (2009).** *On-line expectation–maximization algorithm for latent data models.* JRSS-B. A stochastic-approximation update on sufficient statistics; e-GMM's update is the same, with a different schedule.
- **S. J. Nowlan (1991).** *Soft competitive adaptation: neural network learning algorithms based on fitting statistical mixtures.* PhD thesis, CMU. Decayed sufficient statistics for mixtures.
- **R. M. Neal and G. E. Hinton (1998).** *A view of the EM algorithm that justifies incremental, sparse, and other variants.* Incremental and sparse EM; the free-energy view behind truncated E-steps.
- **M. Sato and S. Ishii (2000).** *On-line EM algorithm for the normalized Gaussian network.* Neural Computation. A discount-factor (EMA) online EM for Gaussian models.
- **P. Liang and D. Klein (2009).** *Online EM for unsupervised models.* NAACL. Stepwise EM; step-size and mini-batch sensitivity in practice.
- **N. Ueda, R. Nakano, Z. Ghahramani and G. E. Hinton (2000).** *SMEM algorithm for mixture models.* Neural Computation. Split-and-merge moves for revival and escaping local optima.
- **G. Hamerly and C. Elkan (2003).** *Learning the k in k-means.* NIPS (G-means). Splitting along the principal component.
- **D. Forster and J. Lücke (2018).** *Can clustering scale sublinearly with its clusters? A variational EM acceleration of GMMs and k-means.* AISTATS. Truncated posteriors for large k.
- **F. Hirschberger, D. Forster and J. Lücke (2022).** *A variational EM acceleration for efficient clustering at very large scales.* IEEE TPAMI. Large-scale GMMs with truncated variational EM.
- **A. Gepperth and B. Pfülb (2021).** *Gradient-based training of Gaussian mixture models for high-dimensional streaming data.* Neural Processing Letters. SGD training of GMMs and avoidance of degenerate solutions.

The defensible contribution is therefore: **scaling laws and component revival for soft mini-batch EM**, validated on real embeddings at ≥ 10⁵ points against tuned baselines.

### Smoke evidence after the fixes

[FINAL-EXPERIMENT-REPORT.md](../../experiments/results/FINAL-EXPERIMENT-REPORT.md) has 48 verdicts: 13 Supported, 6 Contradicted, 25 Inconclusive and 4 "H0 not rejected".

**e-GMM against tuned baselines** (exp_02, 10 seeds, 4 suites; negative means e-GMM is better):
- e-GMM never beats the best standard baseline. H0 is not rejected in any suite.
- **Overlapping-Rotated-Full:** e-GMM beats:
  - online EM: −0.0165, p 0.014;
  - e-means → EM: −0.053;
  - e-means + PostHoc: −0.096;
  - random-init full-batch EM: −0.017.

  It ties full-batch EM started from e-means: +0.0042, p 0.37.
- **Zipf-Separated-Diag and Zipf-Rotated-Full:** within noise of every tuned baseline. The exceptions are random-init full-batch EM, and e-means + PostHoc on Zipf-Separated (−0.0146, p 0.02).
- **Stretched-PowerLaw-Diag:** two baselines are significantly better than e-GMM:
  - full-batch EM from e-means: by 0.0035, p 0.006;
  - e-means → EM: by 0.0029, p 0.03.
- Extending the tuning grids moved no exp_02 baseline: each baseline that had been at an edge tried one more step and kept its setting.

**Revival** (exp_02, exp_03, PoC-2):
- At the old α₀ = 0.35, the no-revival runs collapse. In exp_03, 13.3 of 16 components are dead and the cost is +0.143.
- At a tuned α₀ the effect is small and depends on the data draw:
  - exp_02 Overlap: −0.0126, p 0.002, 10 of 10 seeds (Supported);
  - PoC-2 Overlap: −0.0029, p 0.21;
  - exp_03 Zipf: −0.0045, p 0.47.
- Among revival strategies at tuned α₀ (exp_03), none beats spectral v1 by the threshold. Spectral v1 with count-weighted donors is nominally best: −0.0052 against variance-mass donors, p 0.051.

**Scaling law** (exp_01, PoC-3):
- With T varied and the knees uncensored, every exponent is identified: a_k −0.69, a_B +0.38, a_n −0.20, a_T −0.11. In-sample R² is 0.43.
- 17 of 96 α optima are censored and excluded from the fits.
- The d term makes held-out regret slightly *worse*:
  - LODO +0.00034 in exp_01, over 79 configurations;
  - LODO +0.0007 in PoC-3.
- OOD transfer was tested on only 2–3 configurations, so it is Inconclusive.

**Covariance structure, top-m and finalization** (exp_03):
- Full covariance has the best test NLL (−0.29 against diag) and the lowest training BIC.
- Low-rank + diag beats diag on test NLL (r = 2: −0.079) but never beats full on training BIC.
- Top-m with m = 16 is equivalent to dense within ±0.01 (95% CI −0.0099 to +0.0039). For m ≤ 8 the result is Inconclusive.
- With small n/k, slow-EMA beats last-epoch on test NLL by 0.113. With larger n/k they tie.

**Systems** (exp_04, CPU only):
- The [x², x] GEMM is 41× faster than the naive broadcast: 106 vs 4 398 ms.
- Woodbury is 2.2× faster than Cholesky.
- At a data-mean offset of 1 000, the float32 log-density error is 36 nats uncentered and 1.6e-4 centered.

### Correction to finding B

Finding B says that at α₀ = 0.10, running without revival is within noise of spectral splitting.
- **Where it holds:** on the PoC-2 draw (Overlap −0.0029, p 0.21; Zipf −0.0068, p 0.09) and in exp_03 (Zipf, tuned α₀).
- **Where it does not:** on exp_02's Overlapping-Rotated-Full draw, which splits the rows differently. There, no revival at its tuned α₀ = 0.1 is worse by 0.0126 nats/dim (about 0.20 nats in total) in 10 of 10 seeds, p 0.002.

At a tuned step size, revival helps on some draws by about the practical threshold. It is not the large effect the original PoC-2 showed.

### Still open

- **The torch path has never executed.** The Triton kernel is not wired in, and the torch low-rank and full-covariance paths loop over components in Python.
- **Low-rank + diag** uses an Oja-style subspace heuristic, not an MFA M-step.
- **No multiple-comparison correction** is applied across the 48 verdicts. At p < 0.05, a few Supported or Contradicted lines are expected by chance.
- **LODO p-values are optimistic:** they treat held-out configurations as independent.
- **exp_01 smoke has 2 seeds per curve point,** so H1 is preliminary.
- **All results are at smoke scale on synthetic data:** n ≤ 4 800, sweep d ≤ 64 (OOD d up to 128), and no real dataset.
- **The scikit-learn baseline was skipped** because scikit-learn is not installed.
